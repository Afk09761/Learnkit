🧠 Science-Backed Study Methods

Study smarter, not harder

Decades of cognitive science research have shown these techniques consistently outperform passive re-reading. Master them and you will outperform students spending twice the hours.

01

Active Recall

Closing your notes and trying to retrieve information from memory is significantly more effective than re-reading. Use flashcards, cover-and-recite, or blank-page recall after each study session.

02

Spaced Repetition

Review material at increasing intervals (1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks). This fights the forgetting curve. Use Anki — free software that automates optimal review scheduling.

03

Interleaved Practice

Mix topics within a single study session rather than studying one subject until mastery. Research shows it feels harder but produces significantly better long-term retention.

04

The Feynman Technique

Explain a concept in simple language as if teaching a 12-year-old. Where you struggle to explain, you have found your knowledge gaps. Return to source materials to fill them.

05

Elaborative Interrogation

Ask "why does this work?" and "how does this connect to what I already know?" Linking new information to existing knowledge structures dramatically increases understanding and retention.

06

Concrete Examples

Always ground abstract concepts in specific, concrete examples. When learning osmosis, think of real cells, not just diagrams. When studying economics, use real companies you know.

Time Management

The 2-Minute Rule

If a study task takes less than 2 minutes, do it immediately. Reviewing yesterday's notes, checking one fact, or writing a summary — don't schedule it, just do it. It prevents procrastination from building.

Deep Work

Single-Tasking Blocks

Research shows multitasking reduces output quality by up to 40%. Study one subject with your phone in another room for 25–50 minute blocks. Your comprehension will increase dramatically.

Exam Strategy

Past Paper Mastery

Past papers are your single most important revision tool. Exam boards repeat question patterns. Practise under timed conditions, mark your own work using mark schemes, then analyse every lost mark.

Mental Fitness

Sleep & Exercise First

Sleep is when memory consolidates. Cutting sleep to study more is counterproductive. 7–9 hours plus 30 minutes of exercise daily increases cognitive performance, focus, and emotional resilience.

⏱️ Study Timer

The Pomodoro Technique

25 minutes of focused work followed by a 5-minute break. Repeat 4 times, then take a longer break. Backed by decades of research on attention and cognitive performance.
